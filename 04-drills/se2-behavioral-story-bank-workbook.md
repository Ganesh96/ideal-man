# SE II Behavioral Story Bank Workbook

A structure for collecting truthful examples. This contains prompts, not invented answers. Fill it from real work, school, open source, or personal projects; label scope and outcomes accurately.

## Build a small, deep story set

| Story type | Evidence to recall | Follow-up likely to test depth |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership/delivery | Goal, your responsibility, decisions, result | What did you personally own end-to-end? |
| Disagreement/influence | Different views, evidence, alignment | What if the other person had been right? |
| Failure/mistake | What failed, impact, response, prevention | What signal might have caught it sooner? |
| Ambiguity/prioritization | Unknowns, customer need, trade-offs, de-scope | What did you deliberately not do? |
| Reliability/incident | Timeline, impact, diagnosis, mitigation | How did you separate symptom from root cause? |
| Learning/feedback | Feedback received, action taken, behavior changed | What evidence shows you changed? |
| Cross-team work | Dependencies, hand-offs, conflict resolution | How did you keep the other team unblocked? |
| Quality/helping others | Review, tests, documentation, mentoring | How did you know it helped? |

Use a story for multiple questions only if the same facts genuinely fit. Do not force every story into a leadership slogan.

## Story worksheet

```md
Story title:
Date/context:
Situation (short):
My task/authority:
Constraints and stakeholders:
Options I considered:
Actions I personally took:
Why those actions:
Technical details I can defend:
Dead ends or mistakes:
Result (source and attribution):
What was not measured / limitations:
What I learned or would change:
Likely follow-up:
Truth check: Can I answer detail questions accurately? yes/no
```

## Answer format

**Situation/Task (20–25%)**: enough context and responsibility to frame the example.

**Actions (50–60%)**: your contribution, reasoning, alternatives, collaboration, technical detail, and how you adjusted to evidence.

**Result/Learning (20–25%)**: outcome with attribution and evidence; what changed afterward.

Do not force a metric into every answer. If no reliable number exists, describe observable outcomes and say what was not measured.

## Situational prompt structure: FRAME

1. **Frame:** clarify objective, user impact, authority, deadline, unknowns.
2. **Risks:** correctness, security, data loss, reliability, people, deadline.
3. **Alternatives:** safe immediate containment and durable correction.
4. **Make/communicate:** decide, assign owner, escalate when risk/authority requires, set rollback.
5. **Evaluate:** test/measure, inform affected people, document and retest.

## Practice prompts

- A production change causes a growing error rate. What do you do in the first 10 minutes, and what do you defer?
- A teammate proposes a faster implementation that risks correctness. How do you respond?
- A deadline is fixed but scope is too large. How do you negotiate a safe first release?
- You discover your estimate was materially wrong. Who needs to know and what changes?
- A decision you supported turns out badly. How do you separate reasonable judgment under uncertainty from poor execution?
- A junior teammate is blocked on a problem you could solve quickly. How do you help without taking away ownership?
- Product asks for analytics that may degrade transactional performance. How do you surface options and recommend one?

## Integrity check

Be ready to answer: What did you personally do? What did others do? How do you know the outcome? What would disprove your account? What are its limits? If you cannot support a scale or metric, remove it or label it as estimate/hypothetical.
